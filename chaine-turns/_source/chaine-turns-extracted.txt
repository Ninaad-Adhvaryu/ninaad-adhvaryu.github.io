SOURCE EXTRACT — "How Floor Surface Affects the Rotational Mechanics of Chaîné Turns: A Smartphone
Gyroscope Study Comparing Wood, Rosin, and Marley"
Pulled from Google Drive (fileId 1bzkt_SB7YYxURJKtKKTBVhZlg-zCI52C, docx) via read_file_content, 2026-07-05.
Student author (the dancer herself); keep anonymous per house rule. No institution names in essay body.

CORE
Does floor surface change the rotational mechanics of ballet chaîné turns? One trained dancer, in pointe
shoes, 3 surfaces (plain WOOD, rosin-treated wood, MARLEY vinyl), waist-mounted smartphone GYROSCOPE
(phyphox, 100 Hz), 3 x 10-second trials per surface = 9 trials. |ω| = full-vector norm of triaxial
gyro; integrate |ω| over time / 2π -> turn count. Explicitly EXPLORATORY (single subject, n=3/condition,
NO inferential stats) — a feasibility + hypothesis-generating study.

PHYSICS
Angular momentum L = Iω conserved in the free-spin phase. Push-off angular impulse ΔL = τΔt is bounded
by static friction fs,max = μs·N. Higher μs -> larger push-off torque -> higher ω. Prediction: rosin
(higher friction) -> faster turns. The paper tests whether kinematics reflect this.

RESULTS (Table 1: mean|ω| / peak|ω| / est. turns, mean±SD over 3 trials)
  Wood + Rosin: 4.20 ± 0.32 / 9.14 ± 0.13 / 6.7 ± 0.5   <- HIGHEST mean & turns, but SD 6-8x larger
  Wood:         4.00 ± 0.05 / 9.06 ± 0.23 / 6.4 ± 0.1
  Marley:       3.99 ± 0.04 / 8.77 ± 0.59 / 6.4 ± 0.1    <- SMALLEST SD on mean|ω| = MOST CONSISTENT
- Rosin mean ~5% higher than others = ~0.3 extra turns over 10 s (physically SMALL).
- Peak |ω|: no meaningful separation (rosin 9.14, wood 9.06, Marley 8.77; Marley widest peak spread).
- Turn counts 6.3-7.3, mostly ~6.4-6.7, all BELOW the 7-turn (2520°) reference. |ω| overestimates pure
  yaw by only ~2-3% (adds off-axis components), too small to explain the gap -> the dancer really did
  ~6-7 turns. Trial durations 9.999-10.077 s; ~994-1001 samples/trial (stable 100 Hz). |ω(t)| is
  periodic: peaks = single-leg spin, troughs = step-through; turn period ~0.7-1.2 s.

THE PIVOT (mean vs variance — the interesting part)
The metric everyone reaches for (MEAN speed) says "rosin wins." But rosin's win is (a) small (~5%, ~0.3
turns) and (b) UNSTABLE: rosin SD 0.32 vs 0.04-0.05 for wood/Marley — 6-8x noisier (Trial 2 ran 10-15%
high). Likely because rosin depletes/transfers to the shoe sole unevenly across trials -> friction
changes trial to trial. The metric that actually matters for PERFORMANCE is CONSISTENCY, and MARLEY —
the international professional standard — has the smallest trial-to-trial variability (SD 0.04). So the
mean-focused reading ("rosin is best") misses that Marley's value is repeatability, not peak speed.
Bonus surprise: plain wood ≈ Marley on the mean (4.00 vs 3.99), despite Marley's reputation — but this
is CONFOUNDED (Marley tested in a different studio, at the end of a fixed order). Wanke: 53% of dance-
floor injuries from floors too slippery, 18% from getting stuck — the friction dichotomy that rosin's
variability sits between.

HONEST CAVEAT / CONFOUNDS (this study is caveat-rich — that richness is part of the story)
- SINGLE participant = the student investigator, who KNEW the hypothesis (expectation/effort bias, may
  have pushed harder on the surface she expected to win). n=3/condition -> no inferential stats.
- FIXED condition order (wood, rosin, Marley) -> fatigue/learning confounded with surface.
- Tested on 3 SEPARATE DAYS (12/14/16 March 2026) -> day-to-day fatigue/soreness/learning.
- MARLEY tested in a DIFFERENT studio (subfloor build, room size, temperature not matched) -> any
  Marley difference can't be attributed to surface alone.
- Rosin application NOT standardised (quantity/coverage/method unmeasured; not reapplied between trials).
- Friction coefficients of the 3 surfaces vs the pointe shoe were NEVER measured -> friction-ω link is
  INFERRED, not tested. Waist (not shoe-floor) sensor placement. Smartphone gyro validated for general
  human motion but not for chaîné turns specifically. Rest between trials ~1-1.5 min, timed informally.
=> The friction hypothesis is consistent with the data but not demonstrated; the durable outputs are the
METHOD (a phone can measure this) and the VARIANCE insight.

FUTURE WORK
Multiple dancers at different levels; counterbalanced surface order at ONE shared studio; simultaneous
FORCE-PLATE to test the friction-impulse mechanism directly; standardise rosin by mass/area; add
pirouettes (sustained pivot may be more surface-sensitive than chaîné's push-off).
