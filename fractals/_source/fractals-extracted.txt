CORE
Estimate the fractal dimension D of five geomorphologically distinct Indian coastlines
(Gulf of Kutch, Sundarbans, Konkan, Chilika, Palk Strait) using TWO estimators: box-counting and
the Richardson walking-divider. Motivated by the COASTLINE PARADOX (Mandelbrot 1967, "How long is
the coast of Britain?"): as the ruler shrinks, measured length grows without bound; D captures how a
coastline fills space. Topological dim of a coastline = 1; fractal when D > 1. Britain D ~ 1.25.

METHODS
- Data: Natural Earth 1:10m coastline vectors, clipped to fixed lat/long bounding boxes per region,
  projected to Web Mercator (EPSG:3857; uniform pixel spacing within a small box; NO inter-regional
  absolute-distance comparison), rasterized at 512/1024/2048 px, edge masks (buffer + dilation-erosion).
- Box-counting: count occupied boxes N(eps) on a grid of box size eps; D = slope of log N(eps) vs
  log(1/eps). Grid-origin BOOTSTRAP (128 replicates, random origin shifts per eps) -> 95% CI.
- Scaling band: two-segment piecewise-linear fit (pwlf); report slope of the longer segment as
  segmented D_band (coastlines show scale breaks, not one clean slope).
- Richardson divider: step along the polyline with divider length delta; L(delta) ~ delta^(1-D);
  D = 1 - m (m = log-log slope of L vs delta).
- Python: GeoPandas/Shapely, rasterio, NumPy/SciPy, pwlf. Google Colab.

RESULTS (Table 1; segmented D at 1024 px; Richardson D)
  Gulf of Kutch:  segmented 1.292 (R2 0.999, ~4.3 octaves); Richardson 1.132. bootstrap 1.320/1.275/1.233 (512/1024/2048).
  Sundarbans:     segmented 1.467 (R2 1.000, ~2.9 octaves) — HIGHEST; Richardson 1.342. bootstrap 1.416/1.332/1.268.
  Konkan:         segmented 1.084 (R2 1.000, ~3.7 octaves) — LOWEST; Richardson 1.120. bootstrap 1.232/1.189/1.154.
  Chilika:        segmented 1.413 (R2 1.000, ~3.4 octaves); Richardson 1.175 — BIGGEST estimator gap (~0.24). bootstrap 1.339/1.279/1.233.
  Palk Strait:    segmented 1.369 (R2 1.000, ~3.0 octaves); Richardson 1.102 — LOWEST Richardson; WIDEST bootstrap CI (shallow reef/sandbar, grid-orientation sensitive). bootstrap 1.339/1.274/1.219.
  Segmented range 1.084-1.467; Richardson range 1.102-1.342. All D > 1 (all fractal).

THE PIVOT (what "misbehaves" — and it's the deepest point)
The fractal dimension is NOT one number; it depends on the instrument.
- Richardson divider estimates are CONSISTENTLY LOWER than segmented box-counting (every region;
  Chilika worst: 1.413 vs 1.175).
- Box-counting bootstrap D DRIFTS DOWN with rasterization resolution (e.g. Kutch 1.320 -> 1.275 ->
  1.233 as px 512 -> 1024 -> 2048). Also sensitive to grid placement and scaling-band choice.
=> Meta-paradox: fractal dimension was invented to be the SCALE-INVARIANT summary that resolves the
  coastline paradox (length depends on ruler). But the dimension ITSELF depends on ruler (estimator,
  resolution, grid, scaling band). The number meant to tame ruler-dependence is itself ruler-dependent.
  The honest deliverable is a dimension WITH its method, scaling band, and uncertainty attached —
  not a bare number.

GEOMORPHOLOGY (why the values differ — genuine signal under the method-noise)
Sundarbans mangrove delta (dense tidal channels/distributaries) -> highest D (1.467), most convoluted.
Konkan straight rocky shore -> lowest D (1.084), near-1D. Kutch tidal inlet, Chilika barrier-lagoon,
Palk Strait shallow-reef/sandbar in between. Values near 1 = smooth; near 2 = space-filling.

HONEST CAVEAT / LIMITS
Web Mercator area distortion (relative comparisons only, valid because same projection/approach for
all); rasterization resolution, grid placement, scaling-band width all move the number; only 5 regions;
1:10m data is coarse (sub-decametre structure untested); estimator choice matters. Palk Strait CI widest.
