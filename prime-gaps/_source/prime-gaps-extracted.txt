Conjectures on Finite Ranges"

Student author; keep anonymous per house rule. No institution names in essay body.

CORE
Test two heuristics on prime gaps g_n = p_{n+1} - p_n, using the first 25,000,000 primes
(2 ... 472,882,027, from PrimePages). Two threads:
1. CRAMÉR: normalized ratio R(p_n) = g_n / (log p_n)^2. Cramér 1936: g_n = O((log p_n)^2), i.e.
   R stays bounded as p_n -> inf.
2. HARDY–LITTLEWOOD: relative frequencies of even gaps g are shaped by an arithmetic weight
   (product over odd prime divisors p of k=g/2 of (p-1)/(p-2)).

CRAMÉR RESULTS
- Running max of R(p_n) rises at tiny primes, then goes FLAT across the whole range; stays well
  below the Granville threshold 2 e^{-gamma} ≈ 1.1229 (gamma ≈ 0.5772). (Empirical data up to
  ~10^18 elsewhere puts the running max near 0.9214.)
- Exceedance rates (fraction of gaps with R > threshold) DECLINE steadily as p_n grows; above
  R>0.3 and R>0.4 they approach zero. => large normalized gaps don't get relatively more common.
- Consistent with Cramér AND with Granville's refinement; finite data at this scale CANNOT
  distinguish the two models. Cramér's model has a known flaw (Maier 1985: integers aren't
  statistically independent; the model mispredicts primes in short intervals). Best unconditional
  bound is Baker–Harman–Pintz g_n = O(p_n^0.525), far weaker than Cramér.

HARDY–LITTLEWOOD RESULTS + THE PIVOT
- Gap frequencies decay ~exponentially with a clear PERIODICITY: every third even gap (6,12,18,...)
  is more common; gaps divisible by small primes (6, 30, 210) are "arithmetic favorites"
  (jumping champions). Peaks at g=6 and g=30 match HL weights closely (freq ~1e-2 and ~1e-3).
- **THE PIVOT (Fig 3):** log-log scatter of empirical frequency vs HL weight shows NO trend — points
  form VERTICAL BANDS. Pearson r = 0.0507 (p = 0.616); Spearman rho = 0.0364 (p = 0.719). Near-zero,
  non-significant. The obvious statistic (correlate weight vs frequency) says "no relationship."
  YET the peaks align (Fig 2). Resolution: HL governs the RELATIVE ORDERING among specific gap sizes
  (which gaps are locally favored), not a global proportionality / power law between weight magnitude
  and frequency magnitude. The correlation is the wrong lens; the structure is local peak alignment.
- Residuals (Fig 6): empirical minus normalized HL weight. Largest positive at small gaps
  (g=6 residual = +0.107); cross zero near g≈50; small negative after (g=180 residual = -0.018);
  residual SD = 0.022. A single global scaling factor lambda (≈6.697e-3) can't match both the steep
  small-gap decay and the flatter HL curve — a SYSTEMATIC (not random) misfit. Another case of one
  number failing to capture structure.

THE DEEPER THEME (honest caveat)
Finite computational evidence can only show CONSISTENCY with asymptotic conjectures, never
confirmation (both conjectures are statements as p_n -> inf). Results depend on dataset size,
bin width, normalization, floating-point precision. The near-zero correlation is not a failure of
the theory — it's a failure of the summary statistic to see local structure.

METHOD / NUMBERS
- Datasets: small self-generated (p <= 1,000,000; 78,498 primes) for debugging; large precomputed
  25M primes to 472,882,027.
- Even gaps analyzed up to g = 200; 40 log-spaced exceedance bins; Cramér thresholds 0.1/0.2/0.3/0.4.
- HL scaling lambda ≈ 6.697e-3. Pearson r=0.0507 (p=0.616); Spearman rho=0.0364 (p=0.719).
- Python 3.11: NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib, SciPy, SymPy.
- Related: Goldston–Pintz–Yıldırım, Zhang, Maynard (small gaps); Ford–Green–Konyagin–Tao (large gaps);
  Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan 2016 (unexpected biases in consecutive primes); Banks–Ford–Tao 2023.
