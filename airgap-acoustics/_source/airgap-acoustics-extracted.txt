CORE
Replicate the Fansmitter technique (M. Guri): exfiltrate data from an AIR-GAPPED (network-isolated,
speakerless) computer through an ACOUSTIC covert channel — by modulating a GPU fan between two speeds.
25% duty = binary 0, 100% duty = binary 1 (frequency-shift / on-off keying). Record with a phone
microphone (phyphox app, audio-spectrum FFT). Decode bits with a Python peak-frequency pipeline.
Threat model: an air gap defends against NETWORK attacks, but a compromised machine (malware) can leak
data via PHYSICAL side effects. Four covert-channel families: electromagnetic, optical, thermal, ACOUSTIC.

SETUP / METHOD
- FanControl app; one controllable GPU fan (only one had an audible speed difference). Two duty states.
  25% ~ ~1000 RPM ~ ~67 Hz tone (observed peak clustered ~47 Hz); 100% ~ ~1600 RPM ~ ~107 Hz (~70 Hz observed).
- 5 s per bit (fan inertia + 2 s transition at max 50%/s slew -> slow). 6-bit messages, 30 s recordings,
  5 s preamble to cut timing uncertainty. iPhone 16 mic, straight-line, mic facing the machine.
- Distances 20, 30, 40, 50 cm. 16 trials (4 distances x 4 bit strings). Realistic NOISY shared workspace,
  no noise suppression by design.
- Decoding: load "Peak History.csv" (dominant-frequency-vs-time). Because fan transitions aren't aligned to
  timing, a BEST-FIT search sweeps a decision threshold (from quantiles of the in-band peak distribution),
  a small time offset, and a stretch factor. Split into 6 windows; "vote ratio" = fraction of samples above
  threshold; hard bit = 1 if vote ratio > 0.5. Binary error rate = % of bits wrong.
  Soft error = continuous: closeness = vote ratio (if expected 1) or 1-vote ratio (if 0); soft error =
  100*(1 - mean closeness). Soft penalises confident-wrong more, gives partial credit near the boundary.

THE PIVOT / honest finding
The channel WORKS up close (two frequency bands ~47 Hz and ~70 Hz separate cleanly; 6-bit patterns
recovered) but DEGRADES substantially with distance and under realistic background noise — "inconsistent
beyond short distances," "unreliable for consistent bit recovery." Peak tracker jumps to harmonics / noise
peaks in louder conditions.
SUBTLE POINT: soft error is consistently a few points BELOW binary error at most distances -> even when
the hard decision flips, the vote ratio was often CLOSE to the right side. So confidence degrades
GRADUALLY, not abruptly: the channel "remains partially intelligible even when full bit recovery fails."
The signal still carries some structure even far out. Security lesson: countermeasures must degrade signal
CLARITY itself, not just decoding success. For attackers: partial secrets recoverable in imperfect conditions.

NUMBERS / CONSTRAINTS
- Throughput ~0.1-0.15 bits/s (~5 s/bit + transition) -> only tiny payloads (passwords) realistic.
- Strongly distance-sensitive; environment-sensitive; the fan switching itself may be audible (detectable).
- 16 trials, 4 distances, 6-bit patterns (e.g. '011010'). One fan, single machine, single session.

COUNTERMEASURE (the paper's contribution)
Cheap software mitigation: inject CONTROLLED RANDOMNESS into fan control — small PWM jitter / brief
irregular speed perturbations — to blur spectral separation and weaken timing consistency, without new
hardware. Future work: evaluate countermeasures experimentally; more robust decoding under varied acoustics.

HONEST CAVEAT / SCOPE
Single desktop, single controllable fan, single session, 16 trials, straight-line only (no angles),
noisy but uncontrolled environment. Manual fan triggering -> timing uncertainty (hence the best-fit search).
A replication/feasibility study, not a demonstration of a practical exfiltration tool. The countermeasure
is proposed, not experimentally validated.
