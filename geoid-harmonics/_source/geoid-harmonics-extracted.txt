CORE IDEA
Spherical-harmonic degree truncation of a planetary potential field = LOSSY COMPRESSION. A field on
a sphere expands in spherical harmonics by degree (scale); keeping only degrees <= n_max is like
keeping only the low-frequency terms. Questions: how many coefficients to reach relative accuracy
epsilon, and is degree truncation an efficient way to store them? Compared across fields:
gravity (EGM2008 geoid), geomagnetic MAIN field (IGRF-13), and a secondary CRUSTAL field (NGDC-720).

KEY FINDING / SPINE
Compressibility differs enormously by field, and it tracks SPECTRAL CHARACTER + SOURCE DEPTH.
- GEOID (gravity): error decays SLOWLY. log-log slope -0.861 +/- 0.033. Reaches 1% error at degree
  ~191.2, needing ~37,249 coefficients. (10% at deg 12.1, 5% at 37.5.) Geoid undulations span
  -113 to +101 m relative to the WGS84 ellipsoid.
- MAGNETIC MAIN FIELD (IGRF-13, degrees 1-13): decays STEEPLY. slope -2.924 +/- 0.724. Reaches 1%
  at degree ~5.9 with only 49 coefficients. The degree-1 dipole holds ~93.8% of radial-field power.
- => ~760-fold difference in storage at 1% accuracy (37,249 vs 49); 3.4x steeper slope.
- CRUSTAL magnetic field (NGDC-720): decays even MORE SLOWLY than the geoid. slope -0.248; stays
  above 10% error until degree ~129.5; near-WHITE spectrum. => LEAST compressible.
- THE TWIST: "magnetism compresses better than gravity" holds ONLY for the core/main field. The
  crustal magnetic field compresses WORSE than gravity. Magnetism is not one category.

THE INSIGHT (the payoff — depth -> compressibility)
Compressibility tracks the physical SOURCE DEPTH:
- Deep, dipole-dominated outer-core field -> power concentrated at lowest degrees -> longest
  wavelength -> most compressible (49 numbers).
- Geoid: density structure through crust + mantle -> power across a wide range of degrees ->
  intermediate.
- Shallow crustal magnetisation -> near-white spectrum -> shortest wavelength -> least compressible.
Physical scales: geoid 5% at deg 38 ~ 527 km half-wavelength; deg 12 (~10%) ~ 1668 km (mantle
convection cells); main field 1% at deg 6 ~ 3336 km ~ outer-core radius (CMB ~3480 km). The coarsest
meaningful main-field truncation already captures outer-core-scale structure.
Downward continuation: continue IGRF from surface (a/r=1) to CMB (a/r~1.83): degree-for-1% rises
5.9 -> 13.0, slope -2.92 -> -0.43 (the field "whitens" toward its source).

METHOD / VERIFICATION
pyshtools 4.14.1; EGM2008 (gravity), IGRF-13 epoch 2020 (magnetic main), NGDC-720 (crustal).
Area-weighted relative L2 error e(n_max)=||f - T_n f|| / ||f|| on a Gauss-Legendre quadrature grid
(exact for band-limited), verified analytically via the Parseval degree-variance identity
e^2(n_max) = sum_{l>n_max} sigma_l^2 / sum_l sigma_l^2. Decay summarised by log-log OLS slope with
delete-one-degree jackknife SE. Fully DETERMINISTIC (exact Parseval ratio, no sampling uncertainty).
Grid error matches analytic Parseval to <10^-13 (some checks 2e-14). Extends to Moon (GRGM1200B),
Mars areoid (GMM3), IGRF epochs 1900-2025.

INFORMATION-THEORY LAYER (optional texture)
Two SEPARABLE inefficiencies:
(1) Coefficient inefficiency ~ 0: degree truncation is rate-optimal for isotropic Sobolev classes by
    the Kolmogorov n-width argument; an oracle best-n-term reordering saves only ~0.3 percentage
    points on the geoid at 1% -> the spherical-harmonic basis is essentially coefficient-optimal for
    globally distributed fields. (Slepian/wavelet bases help only for regionally concentrated signals.)
(2) Bit inefficiency (codec-dependent): naive float32 storage sits ~11.3x above the rate-distortion
    floor at 1% error; a water-fill-guided greedy quantizer narrows this to 1.59x, and 1.23x with
    entropy coding. Bit efficiency depends on the codec, not on the truncation degree.
Spectral slope law: e(n_max) ~ K n^{(1-alpha)/2}, so slope p = (1-alpha)/2; Sobolev smoothness index
s* = alpha/2 - 1 is the same quantity. Geoid alpha ~ 2.84 (Kaula-like); main field effective alpha
7.5 (dipole-dominated, band-limited to 13, so an EFFECTIVE index only); crustal alpha < 1 (near white,
slope law doesn't apply). Reference degrees: 360 (geoid), 13 (main), 133 (crustal).

HONEST CAVEAT / LIMITS
Reference degree defines "full signal"; a different reference shifts absolute thresholds (not the
ordering). Geoid 1% threshold mildly depends on degree-2 / ellipsoid + tide-system convention
(deg 191 standard; ~219 if degree-2 excluded). Crustal model itself truncated to deg 133. Compression
ratio counts coefficients only (upper bound on a real codec; bit-level picture is the codec section).
EGM2008 info ceiling ~deg 1787 (per-degree SNR=1) is distinct from the 1% compression threshold
(deg 191, well inside signal-dominated band), so model noise doesn't affect these results.
